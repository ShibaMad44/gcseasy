      Revision methods that work

      Re-reading and highlighting feel productive but don't stick. These do.

      Active recallTest yourself instead of re-reading. Flashcards and exam questions force your brain to retrieve the answer.
      Spaced repetitionCome back to a topic after 1 day, 3 days, then a week. Each return makes it stick for longer.
      InterleavingMix topics in one session instead of doing one topic for hours. It's harder, and that's why it works.
      Past papersDo them timed, mark them with the official mark scheme, then redo what you got wrong.
      BlurtingWrite everything you remember about a topic on blank paper, then check your notes and fill the gaps in another colour.
      SleepYour memory sorts what you learned while you sleep. A late-night cram costs you more than it gains.

      Command words

      The first word of a question tells you what the examiner wants. These mean the same thing in almost every subject.

      WordWhat to do
      State / Give / NameA short answer. No explanation needed.
      DescribeSay what something is like or what happens. No "why".
      ExplainSay why or how. Link your points with "because" and "so".
      CompareSimilarities and differences. Use "whereas".
      SuggestApply your knowledge to a new situation and give a plausible answer that fits the information.
      Evaluate / AssessArguments for and against, then a justified conclusion.
      To what extentWeigh up both sides and say how far you agree.
      AnalyseBreak it down and show how the parts link. In English: how language creates an effect.
      CalculateWork out a number. Show working and give units.
      JustifyBack up your answer with evidence or reasons.

      Use the marks as a guide: a 3-mark question usually needs three creditworthy points, steps or linked ideas, depending on the command word.
