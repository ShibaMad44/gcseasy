Circle theorems GCSE only

  • Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
  • Angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
  • Tangent and radius meet at 90°; two tangents from a point are equal.
  • Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

About 1 minute per mark, with 10 minutes spare to check. Topics marked ✱ are likely to come up on the non-calculator paper: practise them by hand.

Get the method marks
  1. Show every step. A wrong answer with the right method can still score most of the marks.
  2. Write the formula first, then substitute, then calculate.
  3. Don't round until the end. Keep full numbers in your calculator.
  4. Give units and round as the question says (e.g. "to 1 decimal place").
  5. "Show that" questions: write every line, and finish with the exact value given.
Non-calculator tricks
  • Percentages: find 10%, 5% and 1%, then build up.
  • Fractions: ÷ means flip the second fraction and multiply.
  • Estimating: round each number to 1 significant figure first.
  • Long multiplication: use the grid or column method and check with an estimate.
  • Know your squares to 15², cubes to 5³, and the exact trig values.
Common mistakes
  • −3² = −9 but (−3)² = 9.
  • Bearings are measured clockwise from north.
  • Area scale factor is k², volume is k³.
  • In compound interest, use the multiplier to the power of the years: 1.053, not 1.15.
  • Reverse percentages: divide by the multiplier; don't take the percentage off the new amount.
  • Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width, not the other way round.
